About

Ozer Yilmaz is a research engineer at the Ozbolat Bioprinting Lab at Penn State and a PhD candidate at Istanbul Technical University. His work sits where biology meets machine design: extrusion and droplet bioprinting, microfluidics, and 3D in vitro tissue models, together with the custom hardware and software those experiments run on.

He designs printheads that change shape mid extrusion, builds browser based control software for laboratory instruments, and writes the analysis pipelines that turn raw scans into measurements. He holds an M.Sc. in Biotechnology and Bioengineering from the University of Kent and a B.Eng. in Chemical Engineering from Marmara University, and has authored nine peer reviewed publications, two as co-first author.

> 2026-06-14 // nozzle_test.log The Day The Bladders Held Pressure [+]
─────────────────────────

Five relays, two power rails, and one assumption I did not check: that the sensors I ordered covered the range I needed. They did not. The bladders operate between 0.15 and 0.8 MPa and the sensors topped out well below that, so for two weeks every reading was a flat line that looked like a failed design.

When the data looks flat, check the instrument before doubting the design.

With the correct sensors, the first inflation was even across all six lobes, and the extruded filament changed width without pausing the print. That is the behaviour the whole design was aiming for.

// reflection

Two weeks lost to a spec I never verified. The instrument is part of the experiment.

> 2026-05-02 // philosophy.txt Why Every Lab Tool I Build Is One HTML File [+]
─────────────────────────

A lab tool has a short window to prove itself. If a colleague has to install a runtime, create an account, or read a README first, most people go back to the shared spreadsheet.

So: one file, opens in any browser, works offline, syncs when it can reach the network. Booking the biosafety cabinet, counting cells on a hemocytometer, tracking packages, scheduling duties, generating scaffold paths. Same approach, different internals.

The constraint turns out to help. With no build step there is nothing to maintain, and the tools still open two years later.

> 2026-03-08 // recovery.log Secure Boot, BitLocker, And A Laptop That Would Not Wake Up [+]

Changed one firmware setting, rebooted, and got a recovery key prompt for a key I had saved somewhere sensible and then forgotten. The rest of the evening went into EFI partitions.

Noting it for next time: suspend BitLocker before changing Secure Boot. One command, one minute.
